### Pet food

Apply these to cat and dog food, wet or dry.

- Complete or complementary: the label must say "complete" for a main diet. "Complementary" food is a treat or topper, not a main meal. A complementary food sold as a main diet is a serious red flag.
- Life stage: it must match the animal (kitten, adult, senior, or "all life stages").
- Composition: Amazon listings rarely give the full composition or protein %. Find them with web search, for example on the brand's site or Zooplus. Marketing text such as "made with real chicken" does not tell you the first ingredient.
- First ingredient: a named meat or fish ("chicken", "salmon") is better than "meat and animal derivatives" or a cereal.
- Protein: for dry cat food, about 30% protein or more is normal. Below 26% is a red flag.
- Price per kg: compare on this, not on pack price.
- Bag size: opened dry food stays fresh for about 6 weeks, or 7 weeks in an airtight container. Work out how many weeks the bag lasts for the user's animals. A cat eats about 50g to 60g of dry food a day; for a dog, use the feeding guide on the listing. Assume one animal unless the user says otherwise. A bag that lasts 6 to 7 weeks is fine, but say in the report that it needs an airtight container. Never recommend first a bag that lasts more than 7 weeks, however cheap it is per kg. List it lower in the table and say why. A small bag is never a red flag: it only costs more per kg, which the price column already shows.
- Recalls: search for the brand on the Food Standards Agency site and UK pet news. A recall in the last 2 years is serious.
- UK Pet Food (formerly PFMA) membership is a good sign, but it is not required.

Report columns: first ingredient, protein %, complete or complementary, bag size and how long it lasts (for example "7kg, about 4.5 weeks for 4 cats").
